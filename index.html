Comprehensive Grammar Review: Semester 2

1. Parts of Speech & Sentence Complements

TermDefinitionKey Detail/Memory Trick
NounsNames people, places, things, or ideas [3, 4].**"Nouns Name."** Test: Can you put *the* or *a* in front? [4].
VerbsExpress action or state of being [4].**"Verbs are Vital."** If you can change the tense, it’s a verb [4].
Direct ObjectReceiver of the action of the verb [5].Ask: *Verb + What? or Whom?* [5].
Indirect ObjectReceives the direct object [5].Comes before the DO. Try adding "to" or "for" [5].
Predicate NominativeNoun in the predicate that renames the subject [6].Linking verb + noun. Test with an "=" sign [6].

2. Clauses & Phrases

TermDefinitionKey Detail/Memory Trick
Adjective ClauseModifies a noun or pronoun [7].Starts with relative pronouns: *who, whom, whose, which, that* [7].
Adverb ClauseModifies a verb, adjective, or adverb [8].Starts with subordinators (*because, although, if*). Tells *why, when, how* [8].
Noun ClauseFunctions as a noun in the sentence [8].Test: Can often be replaced with the word **"something"** [8].
Appositive PhraseRenames a noun right beside it [9].Example: *Our governor, who has a large income...* [1].
Gerund PhraseVerb ending in *-ing* acting as a noun [10].Test: Can you replace the whole phrase with **"something"**? [10].

3. Sentence Structures

StructureDefinitionKey Detail/Memory Trick
SimpleOne independent clause [11, 12].A single complete thought, even if the subject/verb is compound [11, 13].
CompoundTwo or more independent clauses [11, 12].Joined by a semicolon or a comma + **FANBOYS** [13].
ComplexOne independent + at least one dependent clause [11, 12].Look for subordinators like *because* or *that* [12, 13].
Compound-ComplexAt least two independent + at least one dependent clause [11, 12].The "Combo": Needs a conjunction AND a relative pronoun/subordinator [12].

4. Sentence Functions

FunctionPurposeEnding Punctuation
DeclarativeMakes a statement [14, 15].Ends with a period (.) [14].
InterrogativeAsks a question [14, 15].Ends with a question mark (?) [14].
ImperativeGives a command or request [14, 15].The subject is the "understood you." [14].
ExclamatoryExpresses strong emotion [14].Ends with an exclamation point (!) [14].

5. Sentence Errors & Modifiers

ErrorDefinitionExample/Fix
FragmentAn incomplete sentence [14, 16]."Swimming in the ocean." (Missing subject/verb) [17, 18].
Run-onClauses joined without proper punctuation [14, 16].Two sentences smashed together with just a comma or nothing [16].
Misplaced ModifierDescription is too far from the word it modifies [14, 16]."The senior received his diploma with a buzzcut." (The diploma doesn't have the hair!) [16, 17].
Dangling ModifierThe word being described is missing [16, 17]."Walking across the stage, the diploma was received." (The diploma wasn't walking) [16, 17].

6. Key Agreement & Pronoun Rules

Rule CategoryThe RuleExample from Review
Subject-Verb (Or/Nor)Verb agrees with the subject **closest** to it [19]."Either the students or the teacher **is**..." [20].
Subject-Verb (Each/Every)*Each* and *Every* always take **singular** verbs [19]."Every sock and t-shirt **was** dirty." [20].
Intervening PhrasesPhrases like "along with" do **not** change the subject [19]."Our teacher, along with others, **is** judging." [20].
Pronoun Case (Who/Whom)**Who** is a subject; **Whom** is an object [21, 22]."That’s the girl **whom** I met." [23]. "**Who** sent the card?" [24].
Indefinite Pronouns*Not one, each, anyone* are **singular** [21, 25]."Not one of the students did **his** homework." [25].
