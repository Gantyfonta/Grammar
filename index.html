Grammar Fundamentals: Definitions and Memory Aids

1. Parts of Speech

Term Definition Key Detail/Memory Trick
Nouns Words that name people, places, things, or ideas [1]. Remember: **"Nouns Name"** (people, places, things) [2]. If you can put *the* or *a* in front of it, it’s probably a noun [2].
Verbs Words that express action or a state of being [1]. **“Verbs are vital”** [3]. If you can make it present, past, or future, it’s usually a verb [3].
Adjectives Words that modify nouns or pronouns [1]. They **“add to nouns”** [3]. If it answers *which one? what kind? how many?*, it’s an adjective [3].
Adverbs Words that modify verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs [1]. Often end in *-ly* [2]. Trick: They **“add to verbs”** [2].
Pronouns Words that replace nouns [1]. They **“pro (stand in for) nouns”** [2]. If it replaces a noun, it’s a pronoun [2].
Prepositions Words that show relationships between nouns/pronouns and other words [1]. Think: **"A squirrel and a tree"** (anything a squirrel can do to a tree: on, under, beside) [3].
Conjunctions Words that connect words, phrases, or clauses [1]. **FANBOYS** (for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so) [3].

2. Parts of a Sentence & Complements

Term Definition Key Detail/Memory Trick
Subject The person, place, thing, or idea the sentence is about [1]. Ask: *Who or what is doing something?* [3].
Predicate The part of the sentence containing the verb and telling about the subject [4]. Everything that’s not the subject [5].
Direct Object The receiver of the action of the verb [4]. After the verb, ask: *What? or Whom?* [3].
Indirect Object The noun or pronoun that receives the direct object [4]. Comes before the direct object [5]. Trick: If you can add *“to” or “for”* in front of it, it’s an IO [5].
Object of the Preposition The noun/pronoun following a preposition [4]. Always ends the prepositional phrase [5].
Object Complement A noun or adjective that completes the meaning of the direct object [4]. Look for a noun or adjective after a direct object that completes the thought [5]. (Can be an Adjective [6, 7] or a Noun [6, 7]).
Predicate Nominative (Subject Complement) A noun/pronoun in the predicate that renames the subject [4]. Linking verb + noun that renames the subject [5]. Trick: Replace the verb with “=” to test [5].
Predicate Adjective (Subject Complement) An adjective in the predicate that describes the subject [4]. Look for a linking verb + adjective describing the subject [5].

3. Pronouns (Specific Types)

Term Definition Key Detail/Memory Trick
Personal Refer to specific people or things [8]. The standard “I, you, he, she, it, we, they” [9].
Demonstrative Point out specific nouns (this, that, these, those) [8]. **“Pointing pronouns”**: this, that, these, those [9].
Reflexive Refer back to the subject [8]. Ends in *-self* or *-selves* and reflects back to the subject [9].
Intensive Emphasize a noun or pronoun [8]. Looks like reflexive but you can **REMOVE it** and the sentence still works [7].
Relative Introduce adjective clauses [8]. Introduce clauses: who, whom, whose, which, that [7].
Interrogative Used to ask questions [8]. Question words: who? whom? whose? which? what? [7].
Indefinite Refer to nonspecific people or things [8]. Non-specific pronouns like someone, anyone, everybody [7]. Trick: If it’s vague, it’s indefinite [7].

4. Clauses (Groups of words with a Subject and a Verb)

Term Definition Key Detail/Memory Trick
Adjective Clauses Clauses that modify nouns or pronouns [8]. Start with relative pronouns: who, whom, whose, which, that [9].
Adverb Clauses Clauses that modify verbs, adjectives, or adverbs [8]. Start with subordinating conjunctions (because, although, if, when) [9]. Trick: If it tells *why, when, how,* it’s adverbial [9].
Noun Clauses Clauses that function as nouns [8]. Can often be replaced with the word **“something”** [9].

5. Phrases (Groups of related words without a Subject and a Verb)

Term Definition Key Detail/Function
Prepositional Phrases Start with a preposition and end with a noun [10]. Includes Adjective Phrases (modify nouns; ask *which one?*) [6, 10] and Adverb Phrases (modify verbs; ask *how? when? where? why?*) [6, 10].
Participial Phrases Phrases beginning with a participle used as an adjective [6]. Look for verbs ending in *-ing* or *-ed* used as adjectives [10].
Gerund Phrases Phrases beginning with a gerund used as a noun [2]. Verbs acting as nouns [10]. If you can replace it with **“something,”** it’s a gerund phrase [10].
Appositive Phrases Nouns or pronouns placed next to another noun to identify or explain it [2]. They **rename a noun** right beside it [10].
Infinitive Phrases Phrases beginning with *"to" + verb* that function as nouns, adjectives, or adverbs [2]. Trick: If you can insert **“in order”** before it, it’s probably an infinitive phrase [11].
